# Valoria Audience Psychology & Ethical Engagement Research

## Audience to design for
Valoria's primary audience is capable, ambitious professionals and high performers who know what matters but struggle to convert effort into sustained progress. Common tensions: plateauing, overwork, burnout, fragmented development, and the gap between strategic thinking and consistent execution. Valoria should help them move from capability to clarity, credible visibility, meaningful relationships, and opportunity.

This is not a mandate to maximise time-on-site. The goal is meaningful return visits because each visit helps the professional make progress.

## Evidence base and practical implications

### 1. Self-Determination Theory: autonomy, competence, relatedness
Research-based wellbeing-supportive UX guidelines translate these psychological needs into design heuristics.
- Autonomy: explain choices, let users control pace and privacy, allow pause/resume, avoid coercive flows.
- Competence: provide specific feedback, visible progress, achievable next steps, and explain what scores mean.
- Relatedness: show credible professional community, relevant peers, mentors, collaborators, and shared learning.
Source: Wellbeing Supportive Design – Research-Based Guidelines for Supporting Psychological Wellbeing in UX (2022): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2022.2089812

### 2. Fogg Behaviour Model: motivation, ability, prompt
A desired action occurs when motivation, ability, and a prompt converge.
- Motivation: make the value of an assessment, profile improvement, or learning pathway concrete.
- Ability: reduce unnecessary friction; clarify duration, requirements, save/resume, and what happens next.
- Prompt: contextual prompts tied to a user's stated goal or incomplete task—not generic notification spam.
Sources: Stanford Behavior Design Lab https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model and https://www.behaviormodel.org/

### 3. Cognitive Load Theory and multimedia learning
The website must not make professionals work hard to understand the interface before they can think about their own capability.
- Use clear hierarchy, chunking, concise explanations, familiar controls, and progressive disclosure.
- Keep assessment questions visually calm and answer choices easy to compare.
- Use animation only when it clarifies sequence, status, or meaning; remove decorative distractions.
Sources: Skulmowski & Xu, Understanding Cognitive Load in Digital and Online Learning (2022): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09624-7
Noetel et al., Multimedia Design for Learning (2022): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211052329

### 4. Ethical persuasion and dark-pattern avoidance
Engagement should emerge from value, trust, and agency—not manipulation.
- No fake scarcity, fake activity counters, fabricated testimonials, forced continuity, confusing cancellation, shame-based streaks, or deceptive notification permissions.
- Be transparent about assessment purpose, data use, result limitations, and eligibility.
- Make it easy to pause, resume, export or understand profile visibility settings.
Sources: Trzaskowski, Manipulation by Design (2024): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12525-024-00699-y
Bento-Silva et al., How Dark Patterns Shape Consumer Behavior on Social Media (2026): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijcs.70240

## Translate psychology into Valoria's product

| Audience tension | Experience response | Proof of value |
|---|---|---|
| “I am working hard but not progressing.” | Clear capability reflection and development priorities | Specific PRIME insight and next action |
| “I have plateaued.” | Structured pathways and progress checkpoints | Evidence of new capability, not vanity badges |
| “I am overloaded.” | Calm interface, short sections, save/resume, clear time expectations | Reduced friction and user control |
| “My value is not visible.” | Professional profile built around capabilities and evidence | Credible Registry presentation |
| “I need the right people/opportunities.” | Relevant discovery, transparent criteria, purposeful connections | Clear eligibility and opportunity context |
| “I don't know what to do next.” | One prioritised next action with rationale | Visible progress and meaningful completion |

## Design patterns to test
1. Recognition-led homepage: name the professional tension, then explain Valoria's distinct response.
2. Outcome-led assessment invitation: explain what the taster does and does not establish.
3. Assessment orientation: clear question count, estimated time only when verified, privacy, and pause/resume expectations.
4. Feedback loops: after an action, explain what was saved and what happens next.
5. Dashboard prioritisation: one primary next step, with secondary pathways kept discoverable.
6. Insight-to-action: every result dimension links to relevant development resources or opportunities.
7. Purposeful return visits: saved opportunities, progress updates, relevant events, learning milestones, and profile completeness—only when real and user-controlled.
8. Credible social proof: verified people, outcomes, partner organisations, and transparent dates/contexts. No invented metrics.
9. Progressive disclosure: reveal advanced detail when users need it without hiding important information.
10. Respectful notifications: user-configurable, relevant, frequency-controlled, and easy to dismiss.

## Measurement: optimise for value, not stickiness alone
Track:
- Taster start and completion rates.
- Account bridge completion.
- Formal 58-question assessment start, resume, and completion.
- Assessment save failures and abandonment points.
- Time-to-first-meaningful-dashboard-action.
- VALU report views followed by development action.
- Profile completion and Registry eligibility progression.
- Relevant opportunity saves, applications, and connections.
- 7/30-day return visits segmented by meaningful action.
- User-reported clarity, trust, perceived usefulness, and sense of agency.

Do not use raw session duration or page views as success metrics without evidence of user benefit. A shorter session that completes a meaningful task may be better than a long session caused by confusion.

## Research caveat
These frameworks are design lenses, not proof that a particular interface will work for Valoria's audience. Validate with moderated usability sessions and behavioural data from real users, with consent. Segment feedback by career stage, geography, role, and user intent rather than assuming one universal professional persona.
